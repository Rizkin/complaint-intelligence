## Recurring findings (across Supervisory Highlights editions, Reg E focus)

- Investigations not completed within **10 business days** (or the extended **45-day** windows) without giving
  the required **provisional credit**.
- Unresolved errors closed without the required **written explanation** of the investigation's findings and the
  consumer's **right to request the documents** relied on.
- "Error" defined narrowly by institutions in ways examiners rejected (incorrect EFTs to or from the account
  count; Venable recap of the "error" definition findings).
- Related Reg Z findings pattern: billing-error acknowledgment/resolution clocks not tracked against the
  regulatory deadline itself, only against internal service targets.

**Bank of America specifically** (CFPB actions page, as indexed Oct 2026): a May 2023 multi-agency action
reported at ~$250M total (fake accounts, junk fees, withheld card rewards — with complaint-handling failures
cited among the criticized practices), and a late-2023 **$12M civil money penalty** tied to non-compliance with
a prior order's required policies and procedures. Treat exact figures as reported, not independently verified.

**2026 context, stated honestly:** the CFPB's public complaint-data publication froze after Aug 14, 2026 (this
project's archive is itself the evidence; `docs/01-data-quality.md` issue 1). Whatever the Bureau's operational
future, the underlying statutes (EFTA, TILA, TISA) and the prudential examiners (OCC, Fed, FDIC — see note 04)
persist. For a bank, that argues for **in-house** complaint analytics rather than reliance on the public
database existing.

## What it means for Complaint Intelligence

- **"Missing the clock is itself a finding" is literal, not rhetorical** — the brief's line is the exact
  failure mode examiners write up. H3's claim (cite the deadline-bound section, help the reviewer act inside
  it) maps one-to-one onto the cited findings: 1005.11 provisional credit + written explanation; 1026.13
  acknowledgment/resolution clocks.
- **Golden-set design:** items should include (a) an unauthorized-transfer complaint where the answer must cite
  1005.11's 10-business-day/provisional-credit rule, (b) a billing-dispute complaint → 1026.13, (c) a
  fee-disclosure complaint → 1030, and (d) at least one distractor where no regulation section applies and the
  correct answer says so (over-citation is a failure mode too).
- **Simulated stakeholder material:** the Head of Complaints persona should push back with exactly these
  findings ("examiners write us up for the written-explanation step, not the apology").
- **Positioning:** the demo story is "every complaint shown with the section and clock it triggers" — the thing
  consent orders demand after the fact.

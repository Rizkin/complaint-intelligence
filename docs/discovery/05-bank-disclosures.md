# 05 — How leadership talks about complaint risk (bank disclosures)

Desk-research note 5 of 6 (`W1-DR-01`). Sources: Bank of America 10-K filings and SEC EDGAR. Customer SIMULATED.

## Sources

- Bank of America FY2025 Form 10-K, filed 2026-02-25 (314 pp.); prior 10-K filed 2025-02-25 (306 pp.) —
  investor.bankofamerica.com/annual-reports and SEC EDGAR (CIK 0000070858).
- 10-K structure: Item 3 (Legal Proceedings) incorporates Note 12 (Regulatory and Litigation Contingencies) of
  the consolidated financial statements; Item 1A risk factors carry the standing language that legal and
  regulatory proceedings "may result in fines, penalties, equitable relief" etc.

## What the disclosures say (and don't say)

- Complaints **do not appear as a named operational risk** in the risk-factor structure. The complaint problem
  surfaces two ways: as **regulatory/litigation contingency** (unspecified-amount reserves and disclosures in
  Note 12) and as **regulatory-relationship risk** (examinations, consent orders, CFPB scrutiny — see note 03's
  2023 actions against this bank).
- Translation for the persona: leadership does not ask "how was the complaint triaged?" It asks "what is our
  regulatory exposure, what did remediation cost, and are we about to be written up?" The persona's weekly
  volume/timeliness/emerging-issues pack is the floor beneath those conversations.

## What it means for Complaint Intelligence

- **Sell the risk story, not the efficiency story.** The buyer's own annual report prices complaint failures in
  the contingencies note (fines, penalties, remediation). The flagship's value narrative — surface the
  relief-worthy, deadline-bound complaints first, catch the spike before it becomes a finding — speaks directly
  to that note.
- **Metrics that travel upward:** complaints per $1B assets (already computed: 10.4 for the customer vs peers),
  relief share (42.5% vs ~25% median), emerging-issue deltas vs trailing baseline. These are the shapes of
  numbers that survive the trip from a triage queue to a board pack.
- **Case-study honesty:** the simulated persona reports to Compliance weekly — one layer below where 10-K
  language is written. The demo should show that layer, not pretend to brief the board.
